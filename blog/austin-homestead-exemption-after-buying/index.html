I Bought a Home in Austin This Year. When Should I Apply for the Homestead Exemption?

By Crystal Kilpatrick · November 16, 2026

If the home is now your primary residence, look into the homestead exemption as soon as you own and occupy it. The application is free, but the timing and result can depend on the property, the prior owner’s exemption, and whether you are already claiming a homestead elsewhere.

This is one of the questions I hear most often after a closing: “My tax bill arrived—do I need to do anything?” In the Austin area, the right answer depends on the parcel and the county, but it is worth checking promptly. A homestead exemption can lower taxable value for an eligible owner-occupied home, and it is not something I would assume has followed you automatically from your last address.

Start with owner occupancy, not the tax bill amount

For a Travis County home, the Travis Central Appraisal District says a general residence homestead exemption is available when you own and occupy the property as your primary residence. You cannot claim another homestead at the same time. The district also notes that a recent purchaser may be eligible from the move-in date in limited circumstances, including when the previous owner did not have an exemption and the new owner is not claiming one elsewhere.

That is why I would not use the seller’s tax bill as your answer. Ask whether the property is your principal residence, whether you have moved your exemption from another home, and whether there are any property-specific facts that affect eligibility. If you bought in Hays, Williamson, or another county around Austin, use that county’s appraisal district rather than Travis CAD for the application and its instructions.

What to have ready before you apply

Travis CAD says the application is free and can be submitted online or by mail. Its guidance calls for a driver’s license or state-issued ID with the address that matches the home. Before submitting anything, pull up the property record, confirm the mailing address and ownership information, and make sure you are applying to the correct appraisal district.

The form is an exemption application, not a promise that every part of the tax bill will change by the same amount. Property taxes are based on taxable value and the rates adopted by the taxing entities for that parcel. A lender can help you understand your escrow payment, but the appraisal district and local tax office are the right places to confirm exemption status and the bill itself.

Why this is worth checking now

Texas taxing units generally begin mailing property-tax bills in October, and the normal deadline to pay without penalty and interest is January 31. Travis County’s tax office notes that the deadline moves to the next business day when it falls on a weekend or holiday. If your lender escrows taxes, the lender may receive and pay the bill—but you should still review the property record and the closing statement so you understand what was prorated at closing and what is being paid from escrow.

A new tax bill can also be a useful underwriting check. Compare the parcel’s actual taxing entities, exemptions, and taxable value with the estimate you used when you bought. This is especially important in newer communities or areas with special districts, where a payment estimate based on a broad tax rate can miss part of the picture.

A simple next-step list for a recent buyer

First, verify that the deed and mailing address are correct in the county property record. Next, check whether your lender escrows taxes and review your closing disclosure for the tax proration. Then, if this is your primary residence, go to the correct county appraisal district’s official homestead page and follow its current instructions. Keep confirmation of the application and check the property record later to make sure the exemption appears.

If you are deciding between homes, I would run this same check before you offer: pull the full tax breakdown by parcel, ask about any special districts, and ask your lender to use the actual information in the payment estimate. A lower list price does not always mean a lower monthly carrying cost.

Frequently asked questions

I just bought a house in Austin. Can I apply for a homestead exemption right away?

If you own and occupy the home as your primary residence, check with the appraisal district for the county where the property is located. Travis CAD says recent purchasers may be eligible from their move-in date in limited circumstances, so do not assume you need to wait without checking the current rules for your parcel.

Does the homestead exemption transfer from the seller to me?

Do not assume it transfers. Eligibility is based on you, the home, and your use of it as a primary residence. Review the property record and submit your own application through the appropriate appraisal district if you qualify.

My mortgage payment includes escrow. Do I still need to look at my property tax bill?

Yes. Escrow may handle payment, but you should still confirm the parcel information, exemptions, and tax estimate. Your lender can explain the escrow payment; the appraisal district and tax office can confirm the property’s exemption status and bill.

When are Austin-area property taxes due?

Texas property-tax bills are generally mailed in October and are usually due by January 31. A local deadline can move when January 31 falls on a weekend or holiday, so check the tax office serving the property for the printed due date on that bill.
